# C2 — cold-start, interleaved, repeated: the pager at 8 GB and at 11 GB

**Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Branch:** `feat/qwen3-30b-a3b` · **Status: measurement, one open instrument question.**

This supersedes the 8-token slice in `2026-10-18-slice.md`, whose 2.2× difference was a cache-history
artifact. Protocol here: every run starts with the model file's page cache dropped, arms are
interleaved within a rep with a rotating order, and each arm is repeated 3×. Raw output:
`2026-10-18-8gb-cold.{csv,txt}` and `2026-10-18-sweep.{csv,txt}`.

## The numbers

Qwen3-30B-A3B Q8 (32.5 GB) on the T480 (15.7 GB RAM), prompt p0 (5 tokens), 32 greedy tokens
decoded one at a time against a KV cache, 3 reps.

| config | regions/tok | MB/tok | evict/tok | expert hit | core hit | **physical MB/tok** | ms/tok | tok/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| control (unpaged) | 38.1 *(demand)* | 190.8 | 0.0 | 82.59% | 97.30% | **417.6 ± 48.4** | 5729 ± 378 | 0.175 |
| 8 GB | 108.3 | 542.9 | 104.4 | 66.78% | 97.30% | **395.3 ± 5.6** | 5877 ± 371 | 0.171 |
| 8 GB + sys drop | 108.3 | 542.9 | 104.4 | 66.78% | 97.30% | **1013.7 ± 14.4** | 6805 ± 278 | 0.147 |

Paired against the control, per rep: 8 GB is +148 ms slower (2/3 reps faster, fewer bytes in 1/3);
8 GB + sys drop is **+596 MB and +1076 ms, 0/3 on both**.

The decisive extra arm, same 16-token protocol, one rep each (a budget near the OS's own cache):

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

## What this establishes

1. **The pager loses at 8 GB, and the loss is memory size, not policy.** At 8 GB it fetches
   **2× the demand** (108 vs 54.7 regions/token) — it evicts regions it then needs — reads 1.0 GB/token
   against the OS's 0.42, and is 19% slower. At **11 GB**, where the budget is close to the ~12 GB the
   OS keeps for free, it fetches **54.8 regions/token against a demand of 54.7**, reads 5% fewer
   bytes, and runs 5% faster. So at equal memory the pager **ties** the OS; it does not beat it.
2. **The earlier 2.2× "win" is dead.** The 8-token slice had the control reading 1356 MB/token
   because it ran first with a warm cache; cold-start repeats put it at 418 MB/token. Every arm is
   now reproducible to ±1.4% on the deterministic counters and ±12% on physical bytes.
3. **Reuse is the dominant fact, and it is high.** 73.6–82.6% of a token's experts were already
   resident. Core is effectively always resident (95–97% hit) at both budgets.
4. **A budget below the working set is actively harmful** under `CP_PAGER_SYS=1`: forcing drops makes
   every wrongly-evicted region a future read. That is the honest >RAM regime, and it is where the
   policy has to be good rather than lucky.

## The instrument question this raised (open, must be closed before any absolute byte claim)

Physical reads are consistently **~1.9× the region bytes fetched**, at both 8 GB and 11 GB, in both
arms (control 546 vs demand 274; 11 GB 517 vs fetched 275). It is **not** evictions (11 GB evicts
6.9 regions/token), **not** thread count (1, 2 and 4 threads read the same 337 MB on the 1-layer
file), and **not** what a direct read of the same regions costs: touching six expert regions page by
page after the same cache drop reads **1.24×** their bytes.

What was tested and rejected as the cause:
- **Readahead waste under forced drops.** `CP_NO_READAHEAD=1` (new `MADV_RANDOM` switch in
  `qwen3_map`) cuts bytes by only ~5% and makes the run **2.6× slower** (15.6 s vs 5.9 s/token at 8
  tokens) — readahead is not the amplifier, and it is essential for latency on this SATA SSD.
- **The kernel reclaiming pages the pager counts resident.** RSS peaks at 7.9 GB against a pager
  belief of 8.0 GB, with 12 GB available: the pages are where the pager put them.

So the excess is in the engine's read path (mmap faults over these regions, in this order), not in
the policy and not in the storage. It affects both arms equally, so the **comparison** stands, but
**absolute bytes/token numbers from this engine are inflated ~1.9×** and must not be quoted as disk
traffic until the cause is found.

## What it means for the thesis

At equal memory the pager ties the OS page cache. That is not a defeat, it is the correct baseline:
the thesis was never "a budget beats more RAM". It is that a **region-granular policy can spend a
fixed budget better than a page-granular one** — and at 11 GB there is nothing left for it to do,
because the whole working set fits. The case has to be made where the OS is demonstrably bad:
- below the working set (4 GB, 2 GB), where the OS thrashes and a policy can protect the core;
- with a layout that makes co-selected experts adjacent (Tier B), which the OS cannot exploit
  because it cannot see regions.

That is the next experiment, not a rerun of this one.

## Protocol notes for the next pass

- `--reps 3` with a Latin-square order is affordable (36 min for 3 arms × 3 reps × 32 tokens) and
  should stay.
- The 11 GB arm shows the pager is within 1% of the OS on regions/token there; 12 GB was avoided to
  keep RSS clear of the 15.7 GB machine, and that headroom choice should be stated in any write-up.
- Prefill is 45 s and reads 13.9 GB every run (cold start verified by that number being stable
  across all 9 runs). It is reported separately and excluded from the per-token table.
