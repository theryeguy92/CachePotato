# Tier B on Qwen3: the layout is exact, and co-selection pairing is a loss

**Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Branch:** `feat/qwen3-30b-a3b` · **Status: measured, negative result, mechanism explained.**

The Tier B claim is that expert *placement* can be recompiled per machine: put experts that a token
tends to select together into one region, so two reads become one. The layout machinery is correct
and provably harmless. The layout itself does not pay on this model and disk, and the reason is
quantitative.

## What was built and proved

`models/qwen3-30b-a3b/convert.py --expert-order <json> --group N` reorders experts in the file and
groups N of them per region, touching **nothing else**: expert ids, tensor names and the router's
rows are unchanged. So unlike the v3 permutation experiment (which permuted the router's expert axis
and moved logits by 5.7e-06), this is placement alone and its claim is **bit-identity**.

The pair order comes from `tools/fit_expert_pairs.py`, fitted on a routing trace from a **different
prompt** than the one measured (p2, 64 decoded tokens; measured on p0) — a layout fitted on its own
measurement prompt would be an overfit.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| 1-layer gate (`verify_tierb_grouping.py`) | 128 → 64 expert regions, `.logits/.route/.router` **BIT-IDENTICAL** |
| full file (32.5 GB, 3,507 regions vs 6,579) | `V4 FORMAT: PASS (structure, regions, digests)` |
| full file, 48 layers, p0 prefill | `.logits/.route/.router` **BIT-IDENTICAL to the ungrouped file** |
| fitted pairs | 43 of 64 pairs matched per layer, **co-selection 11.2%** of positions |

## The measurement (cold start, interleaved, repeated; deterministic counters identical every rep)

8 GB (32 tokens, 3 reps):

| layout | regions/tok | MB/tok | evict/tok | expert hit | phys MB/tok | ms/tok | tok/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| one region per expert | **108.3** | 542.9 | 104.4 | 66.78% | 1013.7 | 6805 | 0.147 |
| co-selection pairs | **145.5** | 1413.7 | 144.8 | 61.35% | 1293.7 | 7191 | 0.139 |

11 GB (32 tokens; plain 1 rep, pairs 2 reps — the counters are deterministic):

| layout | regions/tok | MB/tok | evict/tok | expert hit | phys MB/tok | ms/tok | tok/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| one region per expert | **39.6** | 198.7 | 15.7 | 82.24% | 382.6 | 5133 | 0.195 |
| co-selection pairs | **83.6** | 842.3 | 85.4 | 73.84% | 843.5 | 6081 | 0.164 |

**Grouping is worse at both budgets**: 34% more regions and 2.6× the bytes at 8 GB, 2.1× more
regions and 4.2× the bytes at 11 GB, and slower in both.

## Why, in one paragraph

The fitted pairs are co-selected only **11.2%** of the time (mean 7.14 of 64 positions). A region
costs its full 10.0 MB whenever *either* member is selected, so the average fetch brings 10 MB for
~1.7 experts' worth of demand — a ~15% over-fetch per fetch, and twice the bytes per resident
region. Because the budget is denominated in bytes, halving the region count per MB halves the
number of *regions* that fit, which is what determines hits: evictions/token at 11 GB went 15.7 →
85.4 and the expert hit rate fell 82.24% → 73.84%. The shared read saves something only when both
members are chosen, and 11.2% is nowhere near enough to pay for that.

The in-sample estimate said 30.9% fewer regions; the held-out measurement said 34% *more*. That gap
is the overfit warning working as intended — and a reminder that a layout fitted on 64 tokens of one
prompt is a weak predictor even of the next prompt's routing.

## What this closes and what it opens

- **Closed:** co-selection pairing (2 experts/region) on Qwen3-30B-A3B Q8, on this disk, at budgets
  of 8 and 11 GB. It is not a tuning question — the co-selection rate would have to be several times
  higher before the over-fetch stops dominating.
- **Still true:** placement is free. The layout is bit-identical and the machinery is gated, so a
  different layout rule can be measured for the cost of a conversion (4 h on this machine, I/O
  bound) plus one measurement arm.
- **Opened:** the levers the measurements now favour are the ones that change *bytes and reads
  together*, not the ones that trade one for the other:
  1. **core pinning** at 4 GB and 2 GB, where the hot tier is evicted today (expected ~2.6× fewer
     reads at 2 GB);
  2. the **region arena** (`bench/results/c2/2026-10-18-read-amplification.md`), which removes the
     1.9× fault-around over-read *and* makes the budget a real cap — it would cut bytes ~2× at
     unchanged read count, which is the one lever nothing else here achieves.

## Limits

One model, one disk (SATA SSD, ~1.7 ms/read), one prompt, 32 tokens, and a fit from 64 tokens of a
second prompt. The conclusion "pairing loses" is strong for *this* co-selection rate; it is not a
claim about layout compilation in general, and a model whose router concentrates would need the
experiment re-run rather than the result inherited.
