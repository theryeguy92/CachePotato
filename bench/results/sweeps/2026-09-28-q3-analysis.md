## 1. Pre-registered decision rules

### Headline bar — **PASS**

PASS requires the 95% CI lower bound of mean tok/s ≥ 7.0 for `l05`,
`CP_BUDGET=512MB`, `W=4`, greedy, `CP_PAGER_SYS=1`.

| regime | mean | 95% CI | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| battery (committed 09-27, `e2b9f30`) | 8.1296 | [7.4767, 8.7824] | PASS, margin 0.48 |
| **AC (09-28 recheck, n=15)** | **10.7127** | **[9.8792, 11.5463]** | **PASS, margin 2.88** |

The AC figure supersedes the battery one as the headline number. Battery costs
~24% on this machine (same config, same binary).

### Flat-curve rule — **NOT triggered**

The rule asks whether 512MB is indistinguishable from all-resident (736MB);
if so, throughput is compute-bound and the pass is not a memory result.

| | 512MB | 736MB | Δ | Welch test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MoE λ=0.5 | 10.713 | 12.910 | **−17.0%** | p=0.0001 sig |
| MoE λ=0 | 9.212 | 11.071 | **−16.8%** | p=0.0002 sig |
| **dense GPT-2 (control)** | 10.805 | 10.695 | +1.0% | p=0.61 **ns** |

The dense model fits at *both* budgets, so its flatness is the control: measured
in the same session over the same budgets it shows no difference, while the
sparse model shows 17%. The gap is the memory constraint, not machine drift.

**Independent confirmation from bytes moved.** λ=0.5 moves 2.77 MB/token more at
512MB than at 736MB (23,350.3 → 678.8 MB over 8,192 generated tokens), costing
0.01589 s/token. That implies an effective bandwidth of **174 MB/s** — a
realistic SATA SSD figure that accounts for the entire observed gap.

## 3. What the results support, ranked by strength

### ① Memory pressure governs throughput — strong

17% penalty at 512MB (p=0.0001) against a flat control. λ=0.5 swings
7.19 → 13.05 tok/s across 384 → 640MB. The budget is the largest single term.

### ② Locality-trained routing cuts paging I/O — very strong

| budget | λ=0.5 | λ=0 | reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 384 | 152,767 | 251,319 | −39.2% |
| 448 | 64,119 | 88,471 | −27.5% |
| 512 | 23,350 | 40,595 | −42.5% |
| 576 | 6,638 | 10,640 | −37.6% |
| 640 | 2,268 | 2,810 | −19.3% |
| 736 | 679 | 679 | **0%** |

Routing is deterministic, so these numbers are immune to the machine drift that
affects throughput. They reproduce to four significant figures across sessions,
priming states and prefetch windows (three separate `l05` 512MB W=4 runs give
23,032.6 MB / 20,235 evictions / 88.61% expert hit — byte-identical). The
reduction reaches exactly **0% at 736MB**, where both arms are fully resident,
which is the mechanism behaving precisely as designed.

### ③ Sparse + paging vs dense at equal budget — conditional

| budget | dense fits? | λ=0.5 | dense | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 384MB | no | 7.19 | 2.88 | **+149.3%, p<0.0001** |
| 512MB | **yes** | 10.71 | 10.80 | **−0.9%, p=0.83 — tie** |
| 736MB | yes | 12.91 | 10.69 | **+20.7%, p<0.0001** |

The dense model evicts **813,503.5 MB** at 384MB against λ=0.5's 152,767 MB —
**5.3× more bytes per token** (99.30 vs 18.65 MB/token). That is the causal
chain for the 2.5× speedup, and it is clean.

The 512MB tie decomposes exactly:

- both resident: MoE 13.00 vs dense 10.80 → MoE **+20.3%** (compute advantage)
- MoE paging penalty at 512MB: 10.71 vs 13.00 → **−17.6%**
- net: **−0.9%** → tie

**The sparse model wins only where the dense model does not fit.** At 512MB a
475MB dense model fits while a 736MB sparse model does not, and the paging
overhead cancels the compute advantage almost exactly.

Note also that the dense model is paged more coarsely (74 regions, ~6.4MB each)
than the MoE (404 regions, 1.18MB expert blocks), and achieves ~286 MB/s
effective bandwidth against the MoE's ~134 MB/s — the MoE wins on *volume* and
loses on *pattern efficiency*. That gap is the headroom the prefetcher was
meant to capture (see §4).

### ④ Co-design beats bolted-on — moderate

Paired per-prompt comparison at 512MB, within-rep (identical prompts, same
session): **+1.5003 tok/s (+16.3%), sd 2.1733, se 0.5611, t=2.674, df=14,
p≈0.018, 95% CI [+0.297, +2.704]**.

Recorded caveats:

- The measured machine noise floor is **8–17%**, so a 16% effect is at the
  edge of what this instrument can resolve. Only the interleaved design makes
  it usable at all.
- The per-rep advantage declines monotonically (+2.98, +1.61, −0.09). Each rep
  samples a different document block by design, so this is most plausibly
  content-dependence, but with three blocks the magnitude is loose.
- Within each rep the order was always λ=0.5 then λ=0. A systematic within-rep
  slowdown would penalise λ=0 and inflate the result. Future runs must
  **counterbalance arm order**.
- **λ=0 loses to dense by 17.3%** at 512MB (p=0.0009). At this budget the
  router training is the difference between the sparse architecture being a net
  positive and a net negative.

`208bd051d1ebd25f81c12db9037b5e10`.

| rep | arm order | λ=0.5 | λ=0 | paired diff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | l05, l00 | 7.457 | 5.377 | +2.080 |
| 1 | **l00, l05** | 7.114 | 5.014 | +2.100 |
| 2 | l05, l00 | 7.401 | 5.358 | +2.043 |
| 3 | **l00, l05** | 7.267 | 5.410 | +1.857 |

**Pooled paired (n=20): +2.0200 tok/s (+38.2%), sd 0.4932, se 0.1103,
t = 18.317, df = 19, 95% CI [+1.789, +2.251] → PASS** by the pre-committed
rule (CI excludes 0 and is positive). Pooled λ=0.5 7.3098 vs λ=0 5.2899.

Both pre-committed confound checks came back **clean**:

- **No order bias.** λ=0.5-first reps averaged +2.062, λ=0-first +1.979 — a
  0.083 tok/s difference, ~4% of the effect. Counterbalancing removed it.
- **Charging state empirically excluded.** Reps 0-1 ran while charging
  (20.8 W → 2.8 W), reps 2-3 fully charged (0 W). Charging era +2.090,
  full era +1.950 — large in both, and *smaller* when fully charged. The
  confound that forced the original withdrawal is genuinely gone.

### But the anchor-thrash check fired

At 384MB the budget sits only 27MB above the ~357MB hot tier, and that
headroom is **not** enough to keep the hot tier resident under λ=0's access
pattern:

| at 384MB | λ=0.5 | λ=0 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| expert hit | 43.92% | 31.18% | +12.74 pp |
| expert bytes | 170,147 MB | 208,807 MB | −18.5% |
| **core hit** | **98.21%** | **94.07%** | −4.15 pp |
| **core bytes** | **12,375 MB** | **46,145 MB** | **3.73×** |
| total bytes | 54,911 MB | 98,346 MB | λ=0 moves 1.79× more |
| core share of I/O | 22.5% | **46.9%** | |

For λ=0, nearly half of all I/O is **hot-tier thrash**, not expert paging.
Per the rule set before the run ("if core hit rate differs materially between
arms, the result is flagged, not reported"), the +38.2% is a
**whole-hierarchy** effect, not a pure expert-paging one.

**512MB is the clean mechanism test** (core hit ~99.9% for both arms);
**384MB is the whole-hierarchy effect.** Do not merge them.

### Non-monotonicity in the expert tier

The *relative* miss reduction is larger at 512MB (−46%) than at 384MB
(−18.5%). At extreme pressure LRU keeps the universally-hot experts
regardless of routing policy, leaving no room for policy to differentiate;
the *total* effect grows at 384MB only because the hot-tier channel opens.

Cross-budget caveat: the 384 and 512 rechecks used different document blocks
and different rep strides, so per-access byte comparisons *across* budgets are
indicative, not clean. Within-budget arm comparisons are paired and sound.

## 4. Known defects — recorded, not buried

- **The original 384MB arm pair was withdrawn** and has since been
  re-measured (see §3b). The first pair was invalid: λ=0.5 ran while the
  battery was charging (26W tapering to 9W) and λ=0 straddled a 93-minute
  suspend, mostly fully-charged, so the sign of that bias was not determinable
  a priori and could not even be claimed conservative. (`+24.9%` from that run
  is NOT a quotable number.) The replacement run passes, with the
  anchor-thrash flag noted above.
- **Two suspends hit mid-sweep** (13:50:44–14:49:33, 15:35:09–15:45:19), both
  requested manually via `systemctl` from `session-2.scope`. Both landed on
  discarded warm-up prompts, so measured cost ≈ zero — luck, not design.
- **The `W` axis is inert.** W=1 and W=4 produce byte-identical pager stats
  (±0.02%): 23,028.1 vs 23,032.6 MB, 20,231 vs 20,235 evictions. The prefetcher
  reports `0 fetches` in every run — it looks ahead and always finds the expert
  already resident. W=1 vs W=4 is therefore not a meaningful comparison, and the
  earlier "λ's advantage shrinks as W grows" result is not active.
- **The unprimed "sensitivity bound" is inverted.** Unprimed λ=0.5 (11.6015) is
  *faster* than primed (10.7127), while unprimed λ=0 (9.3699) is *slower* than
  primed (10.0432). "Unprimed" does not mean cold — it inherits the previous
  config's warm page cache — so the bound measures state, not regime, and is
  unusable in both directions.
- **Cross-config matrix throughput comparisons are unreliable.** The dense
  control at 512 → 736 (p=0.61) shows the machine *can* be stable, but
  three `l05` 512MB runs with byte-identical I/O span 8.13 / 10.17 / 11.60 tok/s,
  and `l05`/`l00` at 736MB have identical I/O (361.2 MB, 0 evictions) yet
  12.91 vs 11.07 — a 17% gap that can only be machine state.
- **The flat-curve test's two MoE points come from different sessions** (the
  512MB figure from the recheck at ~11:45, the 736MB from the matrix at ~13:35),
  so that verdict rests on the dense control plus the byte-based bandwidth model
  rather than one clean paired comparison.

## 5. What can be claimed

> Q3 **passes** on the pre-registered rule in both power regimes (8.13 tok/s on
> battery, 10.71 on AC, bar 7.0), and the pass is a **memory result**: the
> sparse model shows a 17% penalty at 512MB where a dense model measured over
> the same budgets is flat.
>
> The project has one large, robust, mechanistically-explained result — a sparse
> model under a paging engine is **2.5× faster than a dense model when neither
> fits**, and ~17-21% faster when both fit, with better quality (3.1656 vs
> 3.2633 loss) — and one moderate result sitting near the instrument's noise
> floor: **co-design beats bolted-on**, by **+16.3% at 512MB** (the clean
> mechanism test) and **+38.2% at 384MB** (flagged as whole-hierarchy — it
> includes hot-tier protection, not expert paging alone, see §3b). At 512MB the
> router training is the difference between the sparse architecture winning and
> losing.

**Correction to an earlier framing in this document.** It previously said the
open experiment was extending the budget ladder *downward* (256/192/128MB).
That is wrong, and the protocol already said so at line 103: **budgets below
the ~357MB hot set measure anchor thrash, not routing, and are not a fair test
of the mechanism.** 384MB is already only 27MB above that floor, and §3b shows
the hot tier is under real pressure even there. There is no lower budget to
reach.

The tight regime is tested **offline instead**, by the LRU replay, whose budget
applies to the **expert tier alone** and therefore decouples entirely from the
hot set: λ=0.5 wins at every budget below full residency down to 38MB
(+12.65 pp), converging only at 379MB. What remains genuinely open is the
*throughput* payoff at a scale where the paged tier dominates — which needs a
bigger model (experts are 51.5% of this one), not a smaller budget.
